Atlas, the short answer: Can Shaliach run a company? Yes, the day-to-day operations: inbox, sales outreach, daily posting, video, site fixes and deploys, monitoring and a morning brief. It is not autonomous, by design: every payment, publish and outward email waits for one tap from the owner.

The four businesses

Chairside Reels

A video app (chairsidereels.com). The team fixes and deploys the site, reviews code, watches production and works on sales.

  1. 1 Oct 2026.

    Production went down: the cloud provider declined a card. The team found the cause within minutes and put it in front of the owner; the site came back as soon as billing reopened, and a standing watch now checks it.

  2. Sep–Oct 2026.

    Code review found a critical access bug. QA confirmed it on the live site and it was fixed before any real customer signed up.

  3. Sep–Oct 2026.

    The “Continue with Google” button silently did nothing (a security header blocked the form). Hotfixed the same day; every release now tests that button.

  4. Sep–Oct 2026.

    The sign-up funnel showed people leaving at Google sign-in, so the team built email sign-in. US visitors saw £, so prices now show in the visitor's currency.

Holiday-villa video offer

Property videos for holiday-villa agencies (villas.yairtech.co.uk).

  1. Oct 2026.

    Outreach to holiday-villa agencies in the UK and the UAE: written, sent and followed up by the team, with the owner approving the sends.

Five YouTube music channels

Daily uploads on five YouTube music channels.

  1. Sep–Oct 2026.

    Posting every day without the owner touching it.

  2. Sep–Oct 2026.

    Caught “stress relief” in titles, a health claim YouTube's rules can punish. Re-titled, and the publishing code now blocks it (test written first).

  3. Sep–Oct 2026.

    Caught duplicate titles before they posted, and a dry run caught a blurry Shorts crop before the first Short went up.

Yossi-Yair Edits

Clip channels that cut and post short clips.

  1. Sep–Oct 2026.

    Four clip channels posting 8 clips a day.

  2. Sep–Oct 2026.

    A partner needed his own login: per-user separation was checked and a change-password page shipped, test-first.

Across all four

  1. A morning brief on the owner's phone at 8:00, every day.

  2. A nightly sweep found two working copies with 11 commits that lived on one machine. Everything went to GitHub, and the sweep runs every night.

  3. A public post showed details about the setup that should not be public. It came down, and every agent now follows a rule against it.

What the human still does

He sets the direction in one message to Atlas, the chief of staff. He taps Allow or Not today when an agent wants to pay, publish, log in or email someone outside. When he corrects an agent, the correction is saved as a lesson that every agent follows. That is the whole job, and it is the point: the team does the work, the owner keeps the yes.
